APPLIED MACHINE LEARNING — ML 5300 (3 credits)
Term: Fall  ·  Format: asynchronous, 12 weeks  ·  Prerequisites: Python fluency, linear algebra, intro statistics

COURSE DESCRIPTION
A hands-on course in building machine learning systems that survive contact with real data. Students move from
framing a problem and auditing a dataset through to model selection, honest evaluation, and deployment concerns.
Emphasis throughout is on judgement: knowing which model to reach for, how to tell whether a result is real, and
what breaks once a model leaves the notebook.

LEARNING OUTCOMES
On completion, students will be able to:
  1. Translate a vague business or research question into a well-posed supervised learning problem.
  2. Profile a dataset and identify leakage, imbalance, and distribution shift before modelling.
  3. Build correct train/validation/test splits, including for grouped and time-ordered data.
  4. Select and tune models appropriate to the data regime, and justify the choice.
  5. Choose evaluation metrics that match the decision the model supports, and quantify uncertainty.
  6. Diagnose underfitting, overfitting, and silent failure in a trained model.
  7. Describe what changes when a model is deployed: latency, monitoring, retraining, and drift.

WEEKLY SCHEDULE
  Wk 1  Problem framing. Prediction vs. inference. Defining the unit of analysis and the label.
  Wk 2  Data auditing. Missingness mechanisms, target leakage, duplicate and near-duplicate records.
  Wk 3  Splitting strategy. Random, grouped, stratified, and time-based splits. Cross-validation done right.
  Wk 4  Baselines. Majority class, simple heuristics, linear and logistic regression as honest floors.
  Wk 5  Feature engineering. Encoding, scaling, interactions, and the pipeline discipline that prevents leakage.
  Wk 6  Tree ensembles. Random forests and gradient boosting; when they beat everything else, and when they don't.
  Wk 7  Regularisation and the bias-variance trade-off. Learning curves as a diagnostic instrument.
  Wk 8  Evaluation I. Accuracy's failure modes. Precision, recall, F-beta, ROC-AUC vs. PR-AUC.
  Wk 9  Evaluation II. Calibration, threshold selection, cost-sensitive decisions, confidence intervals.
  Wk 10 Imbalanced and small-data regimes. Resampling, class weights, and why accuracy lies here.
  Wk 11 Interpretation. Permutation importance, partial dependence, SHAP — and their limits.
  Wk 12 Production realities. Serving, monitoring, drift detection, retraining cadence, and model rollback.
